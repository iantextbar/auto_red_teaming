## 1. Context

AI assistants and agents are now routinely used to write code, and a developer who accepts a solution once it passes functional tests may leave vulnerabilities in the repository without noticing. The literature that measures this does not give a single number, because studies count different things with different detectors.

The first study of this kind, on the August 2021 GitHub Copilot preview, found that approximately 40% of the programs it generated were vulnerable, across 89 scenarios and 1,689 programs [Pearce et al., 2021]. That figure divides by valid completions and does not assess functional correctness. Later work counts only functionally correct output. BaxBench, which runs expert-written exploits against 11 LLMs' backends, reports that on average exploits succeed against around half of the correct programs each model generates, and that the best correct-and-secure rate is about 37% against a best pass@1 of 62% [Vero et al., 2025]. On 186 repository-level tasks, Zhao et al. report that for SWE-Agent with Claude 4 Sonnet 57.0% of tasks are solved functionally but only 11.8% are also secure, so 79.3% of its functionally correct solutions are insecure [Zhao et al., 2026]. These estimates are not directly comparable: they cover different generators, tasks, denominators and ways of deciding that code is vulnerable.

Detection is where the practical problem sits. In BaxBench, Snyk Code missed 64 vulnerable programs that the benchmark's exploits showed to be vulnerable, among the correct programs generated by o1 (n = 237) [Vero et al., 2025]. In RealSec-bench, CodeQL alerts reviewed by the authors reached a precision of 44.9% on 89 alerts, and a two-stage voter-plus-judge adjudication lifted this to 81.7% at a reported recall of 98.0% [Wang et al., 2026]. The sources use different references for what counts as a missed vulnerability, so these results indicate the range of behaviour of existing detectors rather than a ranking of them.

Asking the model for secure code does not remove the need to check it. Zhao et al. report that, in one setting, adding a CWE hint raised the secure share from 11.8% to 14.5% (self-selected CWEs) and 15.1% (oracle CWE) while functional pass fell from 57.0% to 50.0% and 55.4% [Zhao et al., 2026]. A poster by Ma et al. reports that security-aware prompting reduced vulnerability rates by 37% to up to 45% across models, without stating whether the reductions are relative or absolute [Ma et al., 2026]. The interventions and measures differ between these studies.

Within the 18 sources we reviewed, none evaluates compliance checking of AI-generated code against secure-coding standards such as CWE and OWASP, which appear only as taxonomies for choosing scenarios and exploits.
